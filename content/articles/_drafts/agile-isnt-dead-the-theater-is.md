# Agile Isn’t Dead, the Theater Is

In the last two years, roles like **Scrum Master** and **Agile Coach** have been hit hard in layoffs. And it’s not just about cutting costs, companies started questioning if these roles actually added value.

At Facebook, Google, Netflix, and others, engineers describe a different picture: no strict sprints, no story points obsession, fewer ceremonies. Instead, they focus on hiring strong people, setting clear goals, and shipping fast.

Does this mean Scrum is useless? Not entirely.
- For **junior or chaotic teams**, Scrum provides guardrails: refinement, planning, and daily syncs help protect focus and improve quality.
- For **senior and autonomous teams**, ceremonies often slow things down—they’d rather align on OKRs and move.

We’re moving from *doing Agile* to *being agile*.
- **Outcomes over rituals**: OKRs and KPIs guide direction better than ceremonies.
- **Autonomy over supervision**: engineers create their own tasks and own the delivery.
- **Feedback over theater**: fast cycles, shipping often, and learning quickly.

**Practical playbook**
1. **Assess your team**: young teams need structure, mature teams thrive with less.
2. **Set quarterly outcomes**: not just sprint deliverables.
3. **Choose the right operating model**: Scrum, Kanban, Shape Up, or OKR-driven flow.
4. **Revisit regularly**: cut rituals if they turn into theater, add structure if quality slips.

Agile isn’t dying. The *ceremony* is. Strong teams don’t need a script—they need clarity, trust, and feedback loops.

What about your team, still ceremony-driven, or already outcome-driven?
